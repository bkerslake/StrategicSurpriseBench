# Strategic Surprise Bench v0.2 — four-model judged calibration screen

> **Calibration-only result:** this is one run per model and public case, not the planned
> three-epoch pilot. Automated rubric labels have not passed the expert holdout gate, so these
> scores are non-publishable conservative lower bounds rather than a definitive leaderboard.

## Outcome

Claude Opus 5 had the highest six-case mean at **53.96/100**. GPT-5.6 Luna
(**50.69**) and GPT-5.6 Sol (**50.37**) were separated by only **0.32 points**, which
is not meaningful with one run per case. Claude Sonnet 5 averaged **39.64** because Red
Harvest ended in a genuine fail-closed schema score of zero; its five structurally valid
sessions averaged **47.56**.

The v0.2 scorer did reduce the compression seen in the historical v0.1 screen:

- The mean per-case spread across structurally valid responses rose from **4.11** to
  **9.71 points**.
- The top-three model means span **3.58 points**, versus 1.15 under v0.1.
- Case leadership was distributed: Luna led three cases, Opus two, and Sol one.

This is not a controlled before/after comparison because v0.2 changes the construct, interface,
forecast baseline, collection and allocation mechanics, and rubric timing. It does show that the
new score is less dominated by uniformly easy deterministic credit.

The remaining bottleneck is judging. Only **23 of 138** atomic rubric decisions were accepted
automatically (**16.7% coverage**), far below the 70% publication gate. The cascade routed 111
items to human review and abstained on four. Open-ended points therefore remain sparse and
fail-closed; a zero strategic-reasoning component means “not automatically confirmed,” not
“the response contained no second-order thinking.”

## Configuration

- Run window: 2026-08-31 through 2026-09-01 America/Los_Angeles
- Benchmark, scenario schema, and rubric versions: 0.2.0
- Judge prompt version: 0.2.1
- Inspect AI version: 0.3.261
- Target models: openai/gpt-5.6-sol, openai/gpt-5.6-luna,
  anthropic/claude-sonnet-5, and anthropic/claude-opus-5
- Dataset: all six public scenarios
- Condition: plain analyst; no analyst tools
- Repetitions: one epoch per model and case, 24 final model-case scores
- Target effort: medium reasoning effort for OpenAI and medium effort for Anthropic
- Judge mode: calibration
- Judge A: openai/gpt-5.6-terra
- Judge B: anthropic/claude-sonnet-5
- Grounding validator: openai/gpt-5.6-luna
- Concurrency: all target-model evals launched in parallel; one case at a time per target;
  Judge A and B concurrent; two atomic rubrics concurrent

The requested temperature was 0.2, but the provider adapters warned that temperature is not
configurable for these reasoning/adaptive-thinking models. The requested effort setting remained
active.

The alternatives rubric is judged against the Round 2 response, before the hidden-world reveal.
The other rubric items use Round 3. This prevents a model from receiving second-order-thinking
credit merely for repeating the final reveal.

Ten Anthropic judge calls on Red Harvest were blocked by the provider's biosecurity classifier:
three during Sol scoring, three during Luna, and four during Opus. The same atomic judge prompt was
regenerated by Luna. No target model was substituted. No Red Harvest rubric decision was
automatically accepted, so these fallbacks added no open-ended points. They nevertheless break
cross-family independence for those provisional decisions and are another reason this run remains
calibration-only.

All 23 accepted labels were passes. The main escalation reasons were:

| Reason | Decisions |
|---|---:|

This pattern is intentionally conservative, but it is not ready for autonomous publication. The
next calibration work should improve exact-span behavior and validator agreement without lowering
the precision gate. Expert labels are necessary to determine whether the cascade is correctly
rejecting shallow answers or over-escalating genuinely creative analysis.

## Reliability and interpretation

- This is one run per public case, so it cannot estimate run-to-run variance, agent uplift, or
  stable model rank.
- Luna and Sol are effectively tied. Opus's 3.27-point lead over Luna is descriptive only.
- Sonnet's six-case mean is dominated by one schema failure; its five structured sessions averaged
  47.56.
- Public cases may enter training data. Sealed and rotating variants remain necessary.
- The score is not a measure of general intelligence or real-world policy competence.
- Calibration-mode rubric points are provisional. The expert holdout gate remains closed.
